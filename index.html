The repo was never the vendor's to hand back

The salary comparison is the easy one; the one that bites at exit is custody. A loaded senior engineer costs $18,290 a month, and everything the engineer writes is work for hire from day one. An agency build runs the opposite way: the source lives in the vendor's organization for the length of the contract, and custody is scheduled as a milestone that clears when the final invoice does.

Put twelve months on each rail. The seat at $5,995 costs $71,940 for the year, 32.8 percent of the $219,480 loaded bill for the senior. A fixed bid at $8,000 a month with a 20 percent source release line holds $19,200 until the transfer clears, 3.2 subscription months spent buying back work the buyer already paid for.

The subscription has no custody milestone to clear. Cards ship to a private GitHub repository named by the client; the first card is committed there and so is the last. Canceling moves nothing, because the transfer was never needed: the work never lived anywhere else. Cancel anytime and keep your code is a workflow fact, not an exit clause.

The cadence fills the repo fast. One active request at a time at the 72 hour claim pace is 86 delivered cards on a 260 weekday year, 86 commits a fixed bid would still be holding at handoff. Month 13 leaves with the same custody month 1 had, and the price of leaving stays $0.
